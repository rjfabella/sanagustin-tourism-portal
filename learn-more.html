About San Agustin

Dive deeper into the rich heritage, unique landscapes, and fascinating facts that make our municipality truly special.

Ridge-to-Reef Landscape

San Agustin is beautifully situated on the northeastern side of Tablas Island, facing the Sibuyan Sea. The municipality boasts a unique "ridge-to-reef" geography that spans approximately 140.48 square kilometers.

From the towering heights of Cabaliwan Peak—which offers views of Mt. Guiting-Guiting on clear days—the landscape descends into lush coastal plains, dense mangroves, and pristine beaches like Long Beach and Kambaye Beach.

  • Terrain: Mountainous in the west, coastal in the east.
  • Climate: Tropical, with distinct wet (May to Nov) and dry (Dec to Apr) seasons.

From Guintigui-an to San Agustin

The area's original name was Guintigui-an, named after the abundance of a local fish called 'tigue'. It was initially inhabited by Negrito and Mangyan tribes before migrants, notably the Montesa family from Romblon Island, settled the region.

During the Spanish Era (around 1635), a settlement was established and the first church was built using coral stone and lime. For a long period in its later history, the town was known as Badajoz.

On June 20, 1957, Republic Act No. 1660 officially renamed the town to San Agustin, in honor of its patron saint, St. Augustine of Hippo.

Eco-Tourism and Agriculture Hub

San Agustin plays a crucial role in the province of Romblon, balancing environmental preservation with economic growth. As an emerging eco-tourism destination, it houses protected marine areas that are vital to national biodiversity conservation efforts.

Economically, San Agustin is an agricultural powerhouse within the province. About half of its workforce is engaged in farming and fishing.

  • Coconut Farming: The primary agricultural driver of the local economy.
  • Cottage Industries: Known for traditional raffia weaving and crafting brooms from tiger grass.

Fascinating Facts

Discover some of the unique things you might not know about San Agustin:

  • The Only Blue Hole: San Agustin is home to the only known "Blue Hole" in the Philippines, a massive underwater sinkhole that draws scuba divers globally.
  • Sunken Spanish Galleon: The waters off Barangay Carmen hold a sunken Spanish galleon, adding a touch of historical mystery to diving expeditions.
  • Festivals: The town vibrantly celebrates the Kalipayan Festival to honor St. Augustine, alongside the Sabwagan Festival in Brgy. Carmen.
  • Language: The primary language spoken by the locals is Romblomanon.
